## Per-chapter content brief

### Ch.1 — Hamilton–Jacobi (`01-hamilton-jacobi`)

- **Focus:** the HJ equation `$H(q, \partial S/\partial q, t) + \partial S/\partial t = 0$` as a PDE for the action `$S$`. Characteristics ↔ Hamilton flow.
- **Math:** for a free particle `$H = p^2/(2m)$` the HJ equation `$\partial_t S + (\partial_x S)^2/(2m) = 0$` separates as `$S = -Et + W(x)$` with `$W = \sqrt{2mE}\,x$`. Recover trajectory `$x(t) = (p/m) t$` from `$\partial S/\partial E = -t + (m/p) x = \text{const}$`.
- **Python:** numerically solve the free-particle HJ for `$E = 1$`, `$m = 1$`, plot `$W(x)$`, then recover `$x(t)$` from the implicit relation. Compare to analytic.
- **Next:** ch.2.

### Ch.2 — Canonical transformations deepened (`02-canonical-transformations-deepened`)

- **Focus:** every Hamilton flow IS a canonical transformation. Infinitesimal CT generated by `$G$`: `$\delta q = \epsilon \{q, G\}$`, `$\delta p = \epsilon \{p, G\}$`. Symplectic group `$Sp(2n)$`.
- **Math:** symplectic form `$\omega = dq \wedge dp$`. Show numerically that the flow of the simple-pendulum Hamiltonian preserves area in phase space.
- **Python:** advect a small rectangle in `(q, p)` under pendulum flow for `$N$` time steps; compute its area at each step (shoelace formula); print constancy to ~1e-6.
- **Next:** ch.3.

### Ch.3 — Integrable systems (`03-integrable-systems`)

- **Focus:** Liouville–Arnold: `$n$` independent commuting integrals → motion on `$n$`-torus. Kepler as canonical example.
- **Math:** for Kepler `$E$`, `$\vec L^2$`, `$L_z$`, `$\vec A$` (Laplace–Runge–Lenz) all conserved. Closed orbits ⇔ extra symmetry.
- **Python:** integrate Kepler `$\ddot{\vec r} = -GM \vec r/r^3$` with RK4, plot the elliptical orbit; print `$E$` and `$L_z$` at start/end.
- **Next:** ch.4.

### Ch.4 — Perturbation theory (`04-perturbation-theory`)

- **Focus:** KAM theorem intuition — most tori survive small perturbation, resonant tori break. Why integrability is fragile.
- **Math:** for the driven pendulum `$H = p^2/2 + (1-\epsilon \cos t) \cos\theta$`, sketch the resonance condition `$\omega_{\rm pend} = n \omega_{\rm drive}$`.
- **Python:** sweep drive frequency over `$\omega \in [0.5, 2.0]$`, integrate the driven pendulum from a small-angle initial condition, record max amplitude; plot amplitude vs. drive frequency — resonance peak at the natural frequency.
- **Next:** ch.5.

### Ch.5 — Continuum mechanics (`05-continuum-mechanics`)

- **Focus:** Lagrangian density `$\mathcal{L}$`, action `$S = \int \mathcal{L}\,d^4x$`, EL in field form `$\partial_\mu (\partial \mathcal{L}/\partial(\partial_\mu \phi)) - \partial \mathcal{L}/\partial \phi = 0$`.
- **Math:** elastic-string Lagrangian density `$\mathcal{L} = (1/2) \rho \dot\phi^2 - (1/2) T (\partial_x \phi)^2$`. EL gives the wave equation `$\rho \ddot\phi = T \phi''$`. Define wave speed `$c = \sqrt{T/\rho}$`.
- **Python:** finite-difference wave equation on a string of length 1, fixed ends, initial Gaussian pulse. Animate one mode by saving 5 snapshots; plot them on the same axes.
- **Next:** ch.6.

### Ch.6 — Classical field theory (`06-classical-field-theory`)

- **Focus:** scalar-field action, EL field equations, Klein–Gordon `$(\Box + m^2)\phi = 0$` as a warm-up. State `$\Box = \partial_t^2 - \nabla^2$` (mostly-minus signature).
- **Math:** EL applied to `$\mathcal{L} = (1/2)\partial^\mu\phi\,\partial_\mu\phi - (1/2)m^2\phi^2$`. Define `$\partial^\mu = (\partial_t, -\nabla)$`.
- **Python:** 1+1D Klein–Gordon: integrate from a Gaussian initial profile, watch dispersion. Plot `$\phi(x, t)$` at `$t = 0, 1, 2$`.
- **Next:** ch.7.

### Ch.7 — Relativistic mechanics (`07-relativistic-mechanics`)

- **Focus:** 4-vectors `$x^\mu = (ct, \vec x)$`, proper time `$\tau$`, relativistic free-particle Lagrangian `$L = -mc^2\sqrt{1 - v^2/c^2}$`.
- **Math:** derive `$p = mv\gamma$`, `$E = mc^2 \gamma$`, `$E^2 = (pc)^2 + (mc^2)^2$`.
- **Python:** for an electron, tabulate `$\gamma$`, `$p$`, `$E$` at `$v/c \in \{0.1, 0.5, 0.9, 0.99, 0.999\}$`. Print as a fixed-width table.
- **Next:** ch.8.

### Ch.8 — Noether in field theory (`08-noether-in-field-theory`)

- **Focus:** continuous internal symmetry → conserved current `$j^\mu$`, `$\partial_\mu j^\mu = 0$`. Energy-momentum tensor `$T^{\mu\nu}$` stub.
- **Math:** complex scalar `$\phi$` with `$U(1)$` phase symmetry `$\phi \to e^{i\alpha}\phi$`: `$j^\mu = i(\phi^* \partial^\mu \phi - \phi \partial^\mu \phi^*)$`. Show `$\partial_\mu j^\mu = 0$` on-shell.
- **Python:** put `$\phi(t, x)$` on a 1D grid as a plane wave `$\phi = e^{i(kx - \omega t)}$`. Compute `$j^0$` numerically and verify it equals `$2\omega$` (analytic).
- **Next:** ch.9.

### Ch.9 — Classical to quantum (`09-classical-to-quantum`)

- **Focus:** Dirac correspondence `$\{f, g\} \mapsto -\frac{i}{\hbar}[\hat f, \hat g]$`. Path integral as a sum over paths weighted by `$e^{iS/\hbar}$`.
- **Math:** `$[\hat x, \hat p] = i\hbar$` from `$\{x, p\} = 1$`. Stationary-phase argument: classical path dominates when `$\hbar$` is small.
- **Python:** heuristic path-integral sum for the free particle: sample 200 broken paths from `$x_0$` to `$x_f$` in time `$T$`, compute `$S$` for each, sum `$e^{iS/\hbar}$` (use `$\hbar = 1$`), compare argument of result to `$m(x_f - x_0)^2/(2T)$` (the classical action).
- **Next:** ch.10.

### Ch.10 — Where to next (`10-where-to-next`)

- **Focus:** closing chapter. Personal-note style. Pointers to symplectic geometry, Floer theory, Arnold's *Mathematical Methods*, geometric quantization, lattice QCD as a concrete numerical application of `$e^{iS/\hbar}$`.
- **Math (light):** one final image — the classical-to-quantum bridge as a 2-line diagram: `$L \to S \to e^{iS/\hbar} \to$ propagator`.
- **Python:** no code, or one tiny line printing the De Broglie wavelength of an electron at 1 eV.
- **Next:** no "next chapter" link — instead one paragraph on where the reader could go.
